# Notice

## What this skill contains

Original work. The criteria, the procedure, the story summary, the severity
levels, the report format, and the fixture stories are our own wording.

The criteria draw on the published sources listed in
`references/criteria.md`. A citation such as `QUS "Unique"` names the
criterion where a reader can find the idea. The citations are references.
They are not quotations.

## What this skill does not contain

No text from any source. The book, the article, and the paper belong to their
authors and publishers and live outside this repository.

## How far each citation was checked

- Lucassen and others (QUS): read from the manuscript that the second author
  hosts, sections 3.1.6 and 3.2.1 to 3.2.4, and Table 1. Nobody compared the
  manuscript with the published journal text. The manuscript does not state
  the journal volume and the year. They come from the citation that the
  skill `story-review` uses. The paper defines five criteria for a set of
  stories. This skill uses four of them and "Unambiguous", which the paper
  lists for one story and also applies to the relation with the other
  stories. It does not use "Uniform", which concerns one template for all
  stories.
- The example of the paper for "Unique" is an epic and a story with the same
  text. The criterion `overlap` excludes a container and its children, and it
  keeps that one case: a child that repeats the container word for word.
- Cohn and Wake: cited for "Independent" as the skill `story-review` cites
  them. Nobody read them again for this skill. Treat each one as a pointer to
  the right section.
- The criteria `coverage` and `traceability` have no published source. They
  are this skill's own. Two more parts are this skill's own: the comparison
  of a story's rule with a requirement under `contradiction`, and the signal
  of two names for one thing under `terms`.
